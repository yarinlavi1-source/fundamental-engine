# Consumer, retail and brands

Separate unit demand, realized price, promotions, category mix, geography and channel.
Compare sell-in to distributors with sell-through to end customers. Inspect channel
inventory, returns, markdowns, replenishment and wholesale/direct channel economics.

For stores: comparable sales definitions, transactions × basket, store openings/
closures, new-unit maturity, occupancy and lease obligations. For brands: repeat
purchase, pricing after promotions, product pipeline and dependence on hero products.
Digital traffic, celebrity endorsements and social engagement are leads, not a moat.

A channel reset may temporarily depress shipments while consumer demand is healthy;
it can also disguise share loss. Seek retail sell-through, competitors, inventory
aging and price realization. Model lower price clearing inventory alongside lower
future gross margin rather than treating the clearance cost as harmless.

Normalize calendar/seasonal differences and extraordinary tax gains. Do not confuse
a gross-margin rebound with better demand. Include marketing required to maintain
the franchise and capital tied up in inventory. Forecast recovery only with measurable
assortment, demand and channel milestones.

Economic model (registry `brain/economics.json`, packet `economic_models`): a brand that
grows by inventing categories is `consumer_innovator` (revenue = sum of categories; a
category that fell at least 5% from its peak and made a new high within four years is
`renewed`; R&D and advertising intensity flat while revenue compounds is the process
evidence, cited by Claude and never an automatic moat rating; a missing category year is
unknown, never interpolated). A subscription network sold to consumers (satellite
broadband, streaming) is `subscriber_network`: revenue = average subscribers x ARPU, and
what is not subscriber x ARPU is named, not assumed.
