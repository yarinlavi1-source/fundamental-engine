# Platforms, marketplaces and advertising

Define both sides of the market and which side pays. Revenue may be transactions ×
take rate or relevant impressions × realized price; engagement is an upstream driver,
not automatically revenue. Gross merchandise value is not recognized revenue.

Investigate liquidity/fulfillment by geography and category, repeat behavior, buyer
and seller incentives, subsidy dependence, fraud/trust costs, multi-homing and platform
policy exposure. For advertising separate impression growth from price/mix, direct
response measurement, advertiser return and demand cyclicality.

Network effects require evidence that an additional relevant participant improves
outcomes for others; user growth alone is insufficient. Take-rate increases can
improve short-term margins while harming participation and retention.

Countercases: disintermediation, alternative discovery channels, regulatory constraint,
content acquisition costs, concentration in large advertisers/merchants and low-value
engagement. Distinguish mature core cash generation from long-dated experiments.

Model operational constraints and unit economics. Cost reductions do not explain
whether platform usefulness or monetization quality improved. Positive engagement
with weak earnings can justify deeper research, not immediate undervaluation.
