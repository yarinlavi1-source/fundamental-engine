# Mandatory adversarial review

Review the strongest material claim before polishing the narrative.
Write the most credible bear mechanism, not a generic list of competition and rates.
For example: apparent backlog growth could be low-quality cancellable orders;
margin recovery could be temporary underinvestment; reported customer growth could
be low-value cohorts; an attractive multiple could use peak-cycle earnings.

For each bear mechanism provide evidence, a competing explanation, financial impact
and a decisive test. Attempt to falsify BOTH the bull and the bear. Do not let a
negative headline outweigh stronger specific evidence simply to appear balanced.

Red-team passes:
1. Evidence: are references original, dated and actually supportive of the claim?
2. Accounting: are definition/period/unit changes masquerading as economics?
3. Causality: can a competing mechanism explain the same observations?
4. Valuation: can growth be funded; are terminal value and ownership coherent?
5. Narrative: are vivid stories crowding out base economics and disconfirming facts?
6. Decision: what new information changes the conclusion, and what merely adds color?

Run research_review after revisions. Do not silently remove challenged claims to
pass gates. If a claim is withdrawn, retain that change in the checkpoint history
and explain why. Unresolved material disagreement belongs in the final conclusion.
