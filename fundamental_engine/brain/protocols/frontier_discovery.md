# Frontier discovery: find emerging value before reported maturity

## Mandate

The user wants quality that is FORMING, not only mature profitable businesses.
Always separate (1) worth discovering, (2) evidence of commercial progress,
(3) attractive ownership at today's price. Negative earnings, negative FCF, high
current P/E or unavailable conventional DCF do NOT remove a company from the
research universe. Unknowns stay explicit and generate tests, not automatic rejection.
Do not lower evidence quality to create more buy signals. Do not promise to find
"the next Palantir" or treat a celebrated CEO's remark as company-specific proof.

## Work in two directions

Top-down: structural change -> increased workload -> constrained resource -> pain
and economic cost -> payer/budget -> solution -> suppliers -> share of economics.
Bottom-up: design wins / production ramps / customer expansion / engineering
milestones -> why adoption is improving -> constraint being relieved -> adjacent
markets -> what the market already expects. Reconcile the two stories.

Start with an industry/theme even if no ticker is known. Use frontier_plan for a
research agenda and discovery_scan for company-specific evidence. No new provider
is needed; use the client's filing/web/technical-report/customer connections.

## Time and geography are part of a bottleneck

For each constraint specify unit, geography, workload, installed versus announced
supply, demand scenario, efficiency changes, construction/qualification time,
substitution and plausible expiry date. Demand/supply ratios are meaningful only
in the same units and scope. A global surplus can coexist with a local shortage.
A scenario shortfall in 2028 is NOT an observed shortage in 2026.

Ask what becomes scarce AFTER today's constraint is relieved. Then ask whether
solving the next constraint reduces this company's revenues. Demand growth can
be captured by a customer, a platform, a low-cost competitor, an open standard or
an in-house design instead of the visible supplier. Shortage is not a moat.

## Required competing explanations

1. More demand and durable supplier pricing power.
2. More demand but rapid capacity growth / price erosion.
3. Efficiency or architectural substitution reduces resource use per workload.
4. Customer insourcing or platform bundling captures the profit.
5. Adoption delayed by integration, budget, trust, regulation or deployment cost.
6. Technical/commercial success with poor per-share returns due to funding/dilution.

A countercase is not a reason to abandon an opportunity automatically. Identify the
observable test that separates it from the bull thesis and its likely timing.

## Evidence of progress before GAAP profitability

Track comparable definitions: paid pilots converting, production shipments after
design wins, repeat orders, deployed rather than announced capacity, utilization,
retention/expansion, contract price and contribution, acceptance tests, sales-cycle
length, renewal cohorts, customer concentration and cash conversion. Hiring,
patents, partnership logos, CEO remarks and TAM claims are weak standalone signals.
They can generate a hypothesis but cannot establish paid adoption.

Use series with matching units/scope/period lengths. Preserve cancellations and
negative evidence, including evidence that contradicts an earlier success. Avoid
hindsight labels: evaluate failed firms alongside winners from the same vintage.
Do not demand evidence appropriate to a mature firm before discovering a pilot.

## Routes and research actions

- explore: a causal hypothesis with testable milestones. Keep it visible.
- monitor_progress: technical/pilot/design-win or demand evidence; seek conversion.
- underwrite_early_growth: paid/production/repeat/scaling evidence plus a mechanism
  of company value capture. Run funding/valuation scenarios without waiting for EPS.
- resolve_counterevidence: preserve both sides and resolve the decisive claim.
- repair_evidence_path: future/unavailable evidence cannot support today's research.

These are workflow categories, not rankings, buy permissions or success odds.
An evidence gate for a priced conclusion does not imply a rejected discovery thesis.

## Valuation handoff

Map demand units, capture/share, realized price and timing into explicit segment
forecasts. Use ramps and delays, not a TAM multiplied by a guessed valuation multiple.
Include a failure/delayed-monetization path and a commercially successful but
shareholder-poor path. Forecast funding through the next milestone and through
scale. Run annual values and stresses; report expensive-but-promising separately
from weak-business and from insufficient-evidence. Avoid binary 'pass/fail' prose.
For material unfunded periods, keep discovery alive but do not publish a funded
fair-value claim. Do not generate position sizes or place orders from this module.

## Output to the user

Explain: what could change; which bottleneck might emerge; why this company could
capture profit; what improved recently; what would disprove it; next dated catalyst;
what today's price assumes; and the dated fair-value scenarios when supportable.
Say when a future bottleneck is a hypothesis. Do not stop at 'AI will grow'.
