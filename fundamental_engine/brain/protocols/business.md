# Explain the economic machine

Write the mechanism before projecting results:
- Offering: what problem is solved and what is the actual alternative?
- Customer versus payer: who uses, chooses and pays? What is the buying cycle?
- Revenue: volume × realized price, customers × net spend, capacity × utilization ×
  tariff, transactions × take rate, or another explicit economic driver.
- Costs: variable cost, fixed capacity, acquisition/retention, servicing, development.
- Capital: inventory/receivables, infrastructure, acquisition and replacement needs.
- Competition: why the customer stays and what could change that decision.

Separate segments/geographies/cohorts when group averages conceal opposite trends.
Build a bridge from driver changes to revenue, margin, reinvestment, cash and shares.
Reconcile the tree with reported totals; do not fabricate volume/price decompositions
when not disclosed. A residual is an information gap, not automatically organic growth.

## Moat test
Identify mechanism, beneficiary, measurable outcome, durability and failure mode.
Switching cost: actual retention and price realization after increases, not merely
complex software. Network effect: more relevant participants improve outcomes after
controlling for subsidy. Cost advantage: comparable service quality and capital cost.
Brand: realized pricing and repeat demand, not follower counts. Regulation/patents:
exact rights, expiry, substitutes and ability to collect attractive economics.

A growing market does not guarantee company share; share gains do not guarantee
incremental returns; incremental returns do not guarantee an attractive entry price.
Produce a concise business explanation understandable without finance jargon,
then state the two or three variables that dominate economics.
