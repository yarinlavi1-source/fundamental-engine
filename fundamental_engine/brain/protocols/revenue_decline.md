# Is the setback temporary, structural or mixed?

Start with the measured decline by period/segment and its magnitude. Keep the price
reaction separate. Construct at least two competing causal explanations.

## Attribution bridge
Test volume, realized price, product/channel mix, FX, acquisitions/disposals,
calendar/seasonality/comparatives, recognition timing and actual demand loss.
Require documented amounts or ranges; disclose unexplained residuals. Never add
hypothetical undelivered sales into historical revenue.

## Recovery conditions
For a delay: identify orders, cancellation rights, delivery capacity, remaining costs,
collection milestones and which later period would recognize the activity.
For a strike/outage: check restart versus throughput, scrap/rework, labor cost reset,
customer churn and whether lost sales can ever be recovered.
For inventory destocking: distinguish distributor inventory from end demand, price
cuts, replacement cycles and structural substitution.
For a divestiture: compare continuing scope, lost profit/cash and sale proceeds;
smaller revenue does not necessarily mean weaker remaining operations.
For FX: constant currency aids comparison, but actual currency cash flows remain real.

## Counter-tests
Compare the firm's explanation with peers/customers and repeated prior explanations.
Seek pricing, retention, cancellations, utilization and cash evidence that would
contradict recovery. A one-time event may expose a fragile business model.
Specify measurable falsifiers and deadlines. Separate closure of the event from
recovery of sales, margin and free cash flow.

Output: event, management explanation, supported/counter evidence, quantified effect,
recurrence, delayed/lost/noncash/unknown effect, recovery requirements, scenarios
and falsifiers. Use the events schema and deterministic normalization bridge.
Never conclude market overreaction from the share-price drop alone.
