# Underwrite potential before conventional earnings

Stages: idea -> prototype -> pilot -> paying customers -> repeat orders -> scaling
-> proven unit economics. Report claimed and evidenced stage separately.
A pilot can fail to become a purchase; purchases can fail to recur; scaling can
make losses worse. No assumed success probability from a prestigious partner logo.

For each opportunity trace:
1. Technical feasibility and reproducible result.
2. Customer need, budget owner and willingness to pay.
3. Contract terms, recurring usage and conversion evidence.
4. Gross contribution after delivery/support and customer acquisition economics.
5. Capacity, supply chain, regulation and distribution needed to expand.
6. Time, cumulative cash burn, capital raise and current-holder dilution.
7. Competitive response and the price already paid for anticipated success.

Size a reachable opportunity from buyer counts, penetration, spend and implementation
constraints. Label assumptions. Top-down TAM is a boundary, not a sales forecast.
Separate market growth, company share and value captured per unit.

Compare failure, delayed adoption, moderate success and major success. Scenarios
are conditional paths, not probabilities. If probabilities are used externally,
require an explicit defensible estimation method and sensitivity; do not invent them.

Milestones need cost, due date, success criterion, failure criterion and source.
Keep the possibility of technical success but poor equity return visible. Negative
profit today does not reject potential; unavailable funding does not disappear
because the addressable market is large.

## Causal underwriting before pricing potential (SOFI lesson)

For every material growth thesis preserve in the dossier:
1. Structural demand change with dated independent evidence and substitution risk.
2. Budget owner, willingness to pay, adoption timing and market definition/units.
3. Why THIS company captures value: alternatives, pricing, retention and distribution.
4. Commercial proof: paid adoption and customer economics, not partnership logos.
5. Per-share economics after reinvestment, regulatory capital, funding and dilution.
6. Strongest competing explanation, falsifier and dated milestone.
7. Explicit forecast driver mapping, plausible range, cost and downside if delayed.

Cross-sell does not prove lower acquisition cost, lifetime value or lock-in.
High gross margin alone does not prove pricing power; test competition and costs.
Registered users / adult population is not an economic market-share estimate.
Annual transaction FLOW is not average reserve STOCK: model turnover and balances.
A launch is not proven cost savings; gross reserve yield is not net profit.
Show annual and sequential changes together; explain one-offs with evidence and
avoid hiding deterioration through a favorable comparison window.
Do not assume industry demand automatically translates into company earnings.
Scenarios reflect evidence and base rates, never a requested analyst target.
Missing proof triggers targeted research, not rejection of early-stage potential.
